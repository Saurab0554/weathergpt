Problem statement

Smallholder farmers face crop losses because weather is unpredictable, and most forecasting tools stop at broad text like “rain expected” or “temperature rising.” That information is not translated into action. In the broader agri-tech landscape, initiatives such as ICRISAT and iSAT have shown the importance of contextual advisory and risk-informed agricultural decisions. Our product addresses the same challenge in a lighter, faster format: weather + crop stage + local context = decision support that is clear enough to act on in real time.

1

Farmer problem

Unpredictable rainfall and heat cause stress during critical crop stages, especially for small farms with limited water access.

2

Forecast gap

Standard weather apps give alerts, but not crop-specific meaning or immediate next steps for the field.

3

Decision gap

Farmers need a simple translation from climate risk to field action, not a long advisory report.

4

Our value

We turn raw weather into a scored risk and an instant recommendation that is easy to understand and act on.

3. Weather data layer: real-time input

This stage connects the app to a live weather source so the advice is based on real conditions rather than static assumptions. It uses a free API (Open-Meteo) to fetch rainfall, temperature, and forecast values for the entered location.

Location lookup

We convert the farm village or district into latitude and longitude using a weather geocoding API.

Rainfall data

We pull daily precipitation totals to detect drought, excess rain, or stress-prone periods.

Temperature data

We use 2m temperature data to capture heat stress and crop sensitivity at each growth phase.

Core logic

The weather layer is fused with crop stage logic to produce a vulnerability score and decision.

4–6. Core vulnerability logic and action generation

The real product advantage is this layer: translating generic weather into crop-stage-specific vulnerability. For example, a flowering cotton crop with low rainfall and high temperatures can face severe stress, while a rice field in a flooded period may be at waterlogging risk. The result is a single risk score and a clear instruction for the farmer.
